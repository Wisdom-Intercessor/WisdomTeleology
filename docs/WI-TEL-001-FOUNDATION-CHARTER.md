# WI-TEL-001 — Foundation Charter
Version 0.1 | Draft for review

## 1. Purpose
Establish a distinct foundational discipline for defining, preserving, examining and revising the purposes and ultimate ends of Wisdom Intercessor and its civilizational vision.

## 2. Mission
Provide an accountable framework to articulate why the ecosystem exists, identify intended ends, maintain coherence among purpose, mission, vision and subordinate objectives, and detect purpose drift. A declared purpose is not treated as proof of truth, feasibility or moral permissibility.

## 3. Domain
WisdomTeleology covers existential and civilizational purpose; mission and vision hierarchy; ultimate, strategic and intermediate ends; direction across time horizons; end-coherence criteria; purpose realization evaluation; mission drift and unintended purpose displacement; accountable purpose revision.

## 4. Five-root position
- Ontology: what exists; concepts, entities, relations and models.
- Epistemology: knowledge, claims, evidence, provenance, uncertainty and revision.
- Axiology: values, rights, ethics, constraints and impacts.
- Teleology: purpose, ends, mission, vision, direction and coherence.
- Praxeology: operational goals, deliberation, decisions, authority, plans, execution and feedback.

Roots are independently owned, connected through contracts. Shared vocabulary is harmonized; repository ownership is not duplicated.

## 5. Principles
1. Purpose shall be explicit and understandable.
2. Ends shall be traceable to approved mission and vision.
3. Purpose is revisable through accountable processes.
4. Purpose is constrained by dignity, rights, ethical safeguards and governance.
5. Evidence and uncertainty inform feasibility and evaluation.
6. Purpose-setting does not grant action authorization.
7. Pluralism and reasonable disagreement are recognized.
8. No purpose statement justifies coercion, discrimination, rights violations or bypassing safeguards.
9. Realization criteria are proportionate and observable where possible; indicators are not the purpose itself.
10. Mission drift and unintended effects are reviewable.

## 6. Scope boundaries
**Included:** purpose records; mission/vision; civilizational ends; strategic direction; coherence analysis; realization criteria; drift reviews; revision controls; interfaces.

**Excluded:** factual adjudication; ontology authority; value/rights definitions; operational command or execution; detailed eight-layer implementation; unsupported metaphysical claims presented as empirical fact.

## 7. Roles (proposed)
Founder/designated architecture authority sponsors changes; root steward maintains domain artifacts; WisdomCore manages meta-architecture, registry and cross-root contracts; Axiology reviews rights and ethical constraints; Epistemology reviews claims and evidence; Praxeology/operational owners assess actionability and execution; affected stakeholders may consult and record dissent. These assignments require formal registration.

## 8. Mandatory controls
Versioned purpose records with ID, author, rationale, scope, status, approval, date and review trigger; traceability to subordinate ends and initiatives; disclosure of assumptions, uncertainty and affected parties; mission-drift reviews; change impact assessment and decision record; separation of purpose approval from action authorization.

## 9. Risks and mitigations
Risks: vague purpose, mission drift, false certainty, value conflict hidden in purpose language, coercive interpretation, short-term displacement of long-term ends, metric gaming, duplicated authority. Mitigations: scope definitions, pluralism, rights review, traceability, evidence limits, documented dissent, review and escalation.

## 10. Acceptance
This root becomes an approved baseline only after owner approval of charter, architecture, requirements and roadmap; registry/interface integration in WisdomCore; boundary checks; and an initial versioned purpose registry. This document is draft, not an approval record.
